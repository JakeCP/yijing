A question of voice

Between sparseness and Legge: two tries at a middle register

The rendering on the site is faithful, but so spare that a reader often cannot tell who acts, why, or how one clause follows the next. Legge is clear because he supplies the implied subject, the logical connectives, and the sense of the technical terms — but in Victorian syntax, with bracketed insertions. This page tests whether we can keep the concrete images and the calm voice, and still give the reader complete, natural sentences. Nothing here has changed the site or data/poem/. Read a few slots closely, then say which strength feels right — or where it over-explains.

乾 Line 1 · 潛龍,勿用。

A — Current: “The dragon lies hidden. / Do not act.”

Middle: “The dragon lies hidden in the deep. / This is not yet the time to act.”

Legge: “…the dragon lying hid (in the deep). It is not the time for active doing.”

For each hexagram below, every slot shows the Chinese first, then four versions side by side. A is the text as shipped. B and C are new. Legge (1882) is shown in full, preambles and brackets included, so the borrowings — and the refusals — are visible.

A — Current

The rendering as shipped (data/hexagrams.json, texts.poem). Fragments allowed; subjects and connectives often left for the reader to infer. Verdicts land as short tags: “Good.” “No harm.” “Misfortune.”

B — Clearer

One light touch beyond A. A supplied subject or a single connective (“but”, “then”, “if … then”) where A leaves a blank; a formula eased only enough to parse. The verse shape, the fixed glossary wording, and the refrain-matching all stay. If B alone satisfies, the revision across all 64 hexagrams is small.

C — Fuller

Closer to Legge’s clarity, still modern and unbracketed. Complete sentences throughout; the if / so / but logic spelled out; technical formulae made understandable where they stand (“hold firm to the course”, “trust is held, and through it the heart goes through”). No new images, no moralising, no turning a verdict into advice.

Legge (1882)

James Legge’s translation, verbatim. The clarity benchmark — and the cautionary tale. Note where his extra words genuinely help (a subject, a “but”, a consequence) and where they invent: a court scene, a motive, a lesson. B and C take the first kind and refuse the second.

What the middle voice promises

  • Complete natural modern sentences — a newcomer can parse every slot without the Chinese beside it.
  • The implied subject and the connective logic the Chinese leaves implicit are supplied, once, in plain words.
  • Concrete images stay at full strength; formulae become understandable in context.
  • Nothing is added that is not in the Chinese: no new landscapes, feelings, or morals; no advice-column “you”.
  • Typically 2–4 lines per line statement. Parallel lines stay parallel, word for word.

A companion proposal: glossing the section labels

Clearer sentences help only if a newcomer also knows what kind of text they are reading. The site currently labels its layers The Judgment · 卦辭, The Image · 大象 (from the later 象傳), Changing lines · 爻辭, and The Lines · 爻辭 (from the bottom). Proposal: keep those names — readers learn them once and keep them — and add a short gloss quietly after each, as modelled in the slot headings below.

NowProposed gloss
The JudgmentThe Judgment · the hexagram’s core statement
The ImageThe Image · a later commentary on its two trigrams, told as a picture
Changing linesChanging lines · the lines that turn into their opposites, and so into a second hexagram
The LinesThe Lines · six short statements, one for each line, read from the bottom up
Line 1 … Line 6Line 1 … Line 6 · one short statement for that line’s place in the figure
用九 / 用六All lines changing (用九 / 用六) · an extra statement, read only when all six lines change

Show the full gloss where there is room (a hexagram page, a first reading); let it shorten or drop where space is tight. The point is a handrail on first encounter, not a permanent subtitle.

A — Current

Heaven moves, strong.
So the noble one keeps strong
and does not rest.

Heaven moves, strong and steady.
So the noble one keeps strong,

Heaven moves on, strong without slackening.
So the noble one keeps his strength up

Legge (1882)

Heaven, in its motion, (gives the idea of) strength. The superior man, in accordance with this, nerves himself to ceaseless activity.

The dragon lies hidden in the deep.

A — Current

The dragon appears in the field.

The dragon appears in the open field.

Legge (1882)

In the second line, undivided, (we see its subject as) the dragon appearing in the field. It will be advantageous to meet with the great man.

All day the noble one is strong, and strong again;

The place is dangerous, but there is no harm.

Legge (1882)

In the third line, undivided, (we see its subject as) the superior man active and vigilant all the day, and in the evening still careful and apprehensive. (The position is) dangerous, but there will be no mistake.

In the fourth line, undivided, (we see its subject as the dragon looking) as if he were leaping up, but still in the deep. There will be no mistake.

Hexagram 3 · ䷂

屯 Zhūn

Sprouting

☵ 坎 Water above · ☳ 震 Thunder below

The Judgment · 卦辭 — the core statement

元，亨，利，貞，勿用，有攸往，利建侯。

A — Current

Begin greatly, and go through.
It is good to hold firm.
Do not set out.
It is good to set up lords.

B — Clearer

Begin greatly, and go through.
It is good to hold firm.
Do not set out yet.
It is good to set up lords.

C — Fuller

Begin greatly, and go through.
It is good to hold firm to the course.
Do not set out on the undertaking yet.
It is good to set up lords to help.

Legge (1882)

Kun (indicates that in the case which it presupposes) there will be great progress and success, and the advantage will come from being correct and firm. (But) any movement in advance should not be (lightly) undertaken. There will be advantage in appointing feudal princes.

The Image · 大象 — a later commentary

雲，雷，屯；君子以經綸。

A — Current

Clouds and thunder: Sprouting.
So the noble one sorts the warp threads.

B — Clearer

Clouds and thunder: Sprouting.
So the noble one sorts the warp threads.

C — Fuller

Clouds gather and thunder rolls: Sprouting.
So the noble one sorts the warp threads, as if ordering tangled work.

Legge (1882)

(The trigram representing) clouds and (that representing) thunder form Kun. The superior man, in accordance with this, (adjusts his measures of government) as in sorting the threads of the warp and woof.

Line 1 · 爻辭

磐桓；利居貞，利建侯。

A — Current

Pacing back and forth.
It is good to stay and hold firm.
It is good to set up lords.

B — Clearer

Pacing back and forth, held up.
It is good to stay and hold firm.
It is good to set up lords.

C — Fuller

He paces back and forth, unable to advance.
It is good for him to stay where he is and hold firm.
It is also good to set up lords.

Legge (1882)

The first line, undivided, shows the difficulty (its subject has) in advancing. It will be advantageous for him to abide correct and firm; advantageous (also) to be made a feudal ruler.

Line 2 · 爻辭

屯如邅如，乘馬班如。匪寇婚媾，女子貞不字，十年乃字。

A — Current

Halting, turning — horses wheeling back.
Not raiders — suitors.
The girl holds firm: not promised.
Ten years — then promised.

B — Clearer

Halting, turning; the horses wheel back.
They are not raiders, but suitors.
The girl holds firm: she is not promised.
Ten years pass, and then she is promised.

C — Fuller

She is held up and turned back; even her horses wheel round.
Those who come are not raiders, but suitors seeking her in marriage.
The girl holds firm and is not promised to them.
Only after ten years is she promised.

Legge (1882)

The second line, divided, shows (its subject) distressed and obliged to return; (even) the horses of her chariot (also) seem to be retreating. (But) not by a spoiler (is she assailed), but by one who seeks her to be his wife. The young lady maintains her firm correctness, and declines a union. After ten years she will be united, and have children.

Line 3 · 爻辭

既鹿無虞，惟入于林中，君子幾不如舍，往吝。

A — Current

After a deer, with no guide —
only deeper into the forest.
The noble one sees early: better to let go.
To go on: shame.

B — Clearer

Chasing a deer with no guide,
he only goes deeper into the forest.
The noble one sees early: better to let go.
To go on brings shame.

C — Fuller

A man chases a deer without a forester to guide him,
and finds himself only deeper in the forest.
The noble one sees the turn early: it is better to give up the chase.
To go on would bring shame.

Legge (1882)

The third line, divided, shows one following the deer without (the guidance of) the forester, and only finding himself in the midst of the forest. The superior man, acquainted with the secret risks, thinks it better to give up the chase. If he went forward, he would regret it.

Line 4 · 爻辭

乘馬班如，求婚媾，往吉，無不利。

A — Current

Horses wheeling back.
Seeking marriage.
Going: good.
Nothing unfavourable.

B — Clearer

The horses wheel back.
Seeking marriage, she goes: good.
Nothing unfavourable.

C — Fuller

Her horses wheel back as she sets out.
She seeks the marriage that is sought of her.
To go is good; nothing is unfavourable.

Legge (1882)

The fourth line, divided, shows (its subject as a lady), the horses of whose chariot appear in retreat. She seeks, however, (the help of) him who seeks her to be his wife. Advance will be fortunate; all will turn out advantageously.

Line 5 · 爻辭

屯其膏，小貞吉，大貞凶。

A — Current

Its fat stored up.
Holding firm in small things: good.
Holding firm in great things: misfortune.

B — Clearer

Its fat is stored up, held back.
Holding firm in small things: good.
Holding firm in great things: misfortune.

C — Fuller

His richness is stored up and held back, hard to share out.
Holding firm brings good in small things,
but misfortune in great things.

Legge (1882)

The fifth line, undivided, shows the difficulties in the way of (its subject’s) dispensing the rich favours that might be expected from him. With firmness and correctness there will be good fortune in small things; (even) with them in great things there will be evil.

Line 6 · 爻辭

乘馬班如，泣血漣如。

A — Current

Horses wheeling back.
Weeping blood in streams.

B — Clearer

The horses wheel back.
Weeping blood in streams.

C — Fuller

His horses are forced to wheel back.
He weeps tears of blood in streams.

Legge (1882)

The topmost line, divided, shows (its subject) with the horses of his chariot obliged to retreat, and weeping tears of blood in streams.

Translator’s notes

  • L2. 字 is betrothal, then childbearing; A and B/C keep 'promised' and do not add Legge's 'have children'.
  • L5. 屯其膏: B stays literal (fat stored up). Legge reads withheld bounty ('dispensing the rich favours'); C meets him halfway with 'richness … hard to share out' — the clearest test in this pilot of how much explaining taste allows.

Hexagram 25 · ䷘

無妄 Wú Wàng

Without Falseness

☰ 乾 Heaven above · ☳ 震 Thunder below

The Judgment · 卦辭 — the core statement

元，亨，利，貞。其匪正有眚，不利有攸往。

A — Current

Begin greatly, and go through.
It is good to hold firm.
What is not right in it brings harm.
It is not good to set out.

B — Clearer

Begin greatly, and go through.
It is good to hold firm.
If it is not right, it brings harm.
Then it is not good to set out.

C — Fuller

Begin greatly, and go through.
It is good to hold firm to the course.
If what is done is not right, harm follows,
and it is not good to set out in any direction.

Legge (1882)

Wu Wang indicates great progress and success, while there will be advantage in being firm and correct. If (its subject and his action) be not correct, he will fall into errors, and it will not be advantageous for him to move in any direction.

The Image · 大象 — a later commentary

天下雷行，物與無妄；先王以茂對時，育萬物。

A — Current

Thunder rolls under heaven; things are given without falseness.
So the early kings, in full accord with the season,
nourished the ten thousand things.

B — Clearer

Thunder rolls under heaven; things are given without falseness.
So the early kings, in full accord with the season,
nourished the ten thousand things.

C — Fuller

Thunder rolls beneath heaven, and each thing is given its nature without falseness.
So the early kings ordered their work in full accord with the seasons,
and so nourished the ten thousand things.

Legge (1882)

The thunder rolls all under the sky, and to (every)thing there is given (its nature), free from all insincerity. The ancient kings, in accordance with this, (made their regulations) in complete accordance with the seasons, thereby nourishing all things.

Line 1 · 爻辭

無妄，往吉。

A — Current

Without falseness.
Going: good.

B — Clearer

Without falseness, he goes.
Going: good.

C — Fuller

He is without falseness, and he goes forward.
His going brings good.

Legge (1882)

The first line, undivided, shows its subject free from all insincerity. His advance will be accompanied with good fortune.

Line 2 · 爻辭

不耕獲，不菑畬，則利有攸往。

A — Current

Reaping without ploughing,
a ripe field without clearing it —
then it is good to set out.

B — Clearer

Reaping without ploughing,
gathering a ripe field without clearing it —
then it is good to set out.

C — Fuller

He reaps without having ploughed for the reaping,
and gathers from a field made ripe without his clearing it.
For such a one, it is good to set out.

Legge (1882)

The second line, divided, shows one who reaps without having ploughed (that he might reap), and gathers the produce of his third year’s fields without having cultivated them the first year for that end. To such a one there will be advantage in whatever direction he may move.

Line 3 · 爻辭

無妄之災，或繫之牛，行人之得，邑人之災。

A — Current

A disaster out of nowhere:
a cow, left tethered —
the passer-by's gain,
the townsman's disaster.

B — Clearer

A disaster out of nowhere: a cow left tethered —
the passer-by gains it,
the townsman bears the disaster.

C — Fuller

Disaster comes undeserved, as when a cow is left tethered:
a passer-by finds it and takes it,
while the townsman bears the disaster.

Legge (1882)

The third line, divided, shows calamity happening to one who is free from insincerity;—as in the case of an ox that has been tied up. A passer by finds it (and carries it off), while the people in the neighbourhood have the calamity (of being accused and apprehended).

Line 4 · 爻辭

可貞，無咎。

A — Current

It can hold firm.
No harm.

B — Clearer

He can hold firm.
No harm.

C — Fuller

If he can hold firm,
there is no harm.

Legge (1882)

The fourth line, undivided, shows (a case) in which, if its subject can remain firm and correct, there will be no error.

Line 5 · 爻辭

無妄之疾，勿藥有喜。

A — Current

Sickness out of nowhere:
no medicine — there will be joy.

B — Clearer

Sickness out of nowhere:
take no medicine, and there will be joy.

C — Fuller

Sickness comes undeserved.
Take no medicine for it, and there will be joy.

Legge (1882)

The fifth line, undivided, shows one who is free from insincerity, and yet has fallen ill. Let him not use medicine, and he will have occasion for joy (in his recovery).

Line 6 · 爻辭

無妄，行有眚，無攸利。

A — Current

Without falseness,
going brings harm.
No good anywhere.

B — Clearer

Without falseness, but going brings harm.
No good anywhere.

C — Fuller

He is without falseness, yet if he acts, harm follows.
There is no good in any direction.

Legge (1882)

The topmost line, undivided, shows its subject free from insincerity, yet sure to fall into error, if he take action. (His action) will not be advantageous in any way.

Translator’s notes

  • L2. 菑畬 is newly cleared ground and a field in its third year; B/C keep the paradox plain and do not turn it into a farming note.
  • L3. Legge adds that the neighbours are 'accused and apprehended'; C leaves the townsman's disaster open, as the Chinese does.

Hexagram 29 · ䷜

坎 Kǎn

Pit

☵ 坎 Water above · ☵ 坎 Water below

The Judgment · 卦辭 — the core statement

習坎，有孚，維心亨，行有尚。

A — Current

Pit after pit.
There is trust.
Only the heart goes through.
Going is honoured.

B — Clearer

Pit after pit.
There is trust; the heart goes through.
Going is honoured.

C — Fuller

Pit after pit, the danger repeated.
Trust is held, and through it the heart goes through.
Action taken from that trust is honoured.

Legge (1882)

Khan, here repeated, shows the possession of sincerity, through which the mind is penetrating. Action (in accordance with this) will be of high value.

The Image · 大象 — a later commentary

水洊至，習坎；君子以常德行，習教事。

A — Current

Water comes again and again: Pit after pit.
So the noble one holds virtue and conduct constant
and practises teaching.

B — Clearer

Water comes again and again: Pit after pit.
So the noble one holds virtue and conduct constant,
and practises teaching.

C — Fuller

Water flows on and comes again: Pit after pit.
So the noble one keeps his virtue and his conduct constant,
and keeps up the practice of teaching.

Legge (1882)

(The representation of) water flowing on continuously forms the repeated Khan. The superior man, in accordance with this, maintains constantly the virtue (of his heart) and (the integrity of) his conduct, and practises the business of instruction.

Line 1 · 爻辭

習坎，入于坎窞，凶。

A — Current

Pit after pit —
down into a pit inside the pit.
Misfortune.

B — Clearer

Pit after pit: down into a pit inside the pit.
Misfortune.

C — Fuller

In the repeated pit, he goes down into a still deeper pit within it.
Misfortune.

Legge (1882)

The first line, divided, shows its subject in the double defile, and (yet) entering a cavern within it. There will be evil.

Line 2 · 爻辭

坎有險，求小得。

A — Current

The pit is perilous.
Seeking brings a little.

B — Clearer

The pit is perilous.
Seeking, he gets a little of what he seeks.

C — Fuller

He is in peril in the pit.
He seeks a way out, and gets a little of what he seeks.

Legge (1882)

The second line, undivided, shows its subject in all the peril of the defile. He will, however, get a little (of the deliverance) that he seeks.

Line 3 · 爻辭

來之坎坎，險且枕，入于坎窞，勿用。

A — Current

Coming, going: pit, pit.
Peril for a pillow.
Down into a pit inside the pit.
Do not act.

B — Clearer

Coming or going, pit after pit;
peril is his pillow, and he sinks deeper.
Do not act.

C — Fuller

Whether he comes or goes, he meets pit after pit.
All is peril and unrest, and he sinks into the deeper pit.
In such a case, do not act.

Legge (1882)

The third line, divided, shows its subject, whether he comes or goes (=descends or ascends), confronted by a defile. All is peril to him and unrest. (His endeavours) will lead him into the cavern of the pit. There should be no action (in such a case).

Line 4 · 爻辭

樽酒簋貳，用缶，納約自牖，終無咎。

A — Current

A jar of wine, two vessels of grain —
earthenware only —
the simple gift passed in at the window.
In the end, no harm.

B — Clearer

A jar of wine and two vessels of grain, in plain earthenware —
the simple gift passed in at the window.
In the end, no harm.

C — Fuller

A jar of wine and two vessels of grain, served in plain earthenware;
the simple gift is passed in through the window.
In the end there is no harm.

Legge (1882)

The fourth line, divided, shows its subject (at a feast), with (simply) a bottle of spirits, and a subsidiary basket of rice, while (the cups and bowls) are (only) of earthenware. He introduces his important lessons (as his ruler’s) intelligence admits. There will in the end be no error.

Line 5 · 爻辭

坎不盈，祗既平，無咎。

A — Current

The pit, not overfull,
only just level.
No harm.

B — Clearer

The water in the pit is not overfull;
it is only just level.
No harm.

C — Fuller

The water in the pit does not overflow;
it has only just come level.
No harm.

Legge (1882)

The fifth line, undivided, shows the water of the defile not yet full, (so that it might flow away); but order will (soon) be brought about. There will be no error.

Line 6 · 爻辭

係用徽纆，寘于叢棘，三歲不得，凶。

A — Current

Bound with cords,
set down among thorns,
three years, no way out.
Misfortune.

B — Clearer

Bound with cords and set down among thorns,
for three years he finds no way out.
Misfortune.

C — Fuller

Bound with cords and set down in a thicket of thorns,
in three years he does not find his way out.
Misfortune.

Legge (1882)

The topmost line, divided, shows its subject bound with cords of three strands or two strands, and placed in the thicket of thorns. But in three years he does not learn the course for him to pursue. There will be evil.

Translator’s notes

  • judgment. 維心亨: B links trust and heart with a semicolon; C spells out Legge's 'through which', still in one calm sentence.
  • L4. Legge's 'important lessons' is his gloss on 納約; B/C keep the simple gift and the window.

Hexagram 55 · ䷶

豐 Fēng

Fullness

☳ 震 Thunder above · ☲ 離 Fire below

The Judgment · 卦辭 — the core statement

亨，王假之，勿憂，宜日中。

A — Current

It goes through.
The king comes to it.
Do not grieve:
it should be as at midday.

B — Clearer

It goes through; the king comes to it.
Do not grieve.
Let it stand as at midday.

C — Fuller

It goes through; the king attains this fullness.
There is no cause to grieve.
Let him be as the sun at midday.

Legge (1882)

Fang intimates progress and development. When a king has reached the point (which the name denotes) there is no occasion to be anxious (through fear of a change). Let him be as the sun at noon.

The Image · 大象 — a later commentary

雷電皆至，豐；君子以折獄致刑。

A — Current

Thunder and lightning both arrive: Fullness.
So the noble one settles lawsuits,
and carries out punishments.

B — Clearer

The Judgment · 卦辭 — the core statement

亨，利貞，可小事，不可大事。飛鳥遺之音，不宜上宜下，大吉。

A — Current

It goes through.
It is good to hold firm.
Small things can be done; great things cannot.
A flying bird leaves its sound behind:
not fit to go up, fit to go down. Great good.

B — Clearer

It goes through. It is good to hold firm.
Small things can be done; great things cannot.
A flying bird leaves its sound behind: fit to go down, not up.
Great good.

C — Fuller

It goes through, and it is good to hold firm to the course.
This can be done in small affairs, but not in great ones.
Like the sound a flying bird leaves behind it as it comes down:
to descend is fitting, to ascend is not. Great good.

Legge (1882)

Hsiao Kwo indicates that (in the circumstances which it implies) there will be progress and attainment. But it will be advantageous to be firm and correct. (What the name denotes) may be done in small affairs, but not in great affairs. (It is like) the notes that come down from a bird on the wing;—to descend is better than to ascend. There will (in this way) be great good fortune.

Translator’s notes

How to judge the pilot

Three useful questions, slot by slot. First: in B or C, can you say who acts and why one clause follows the next, without rereading? Second: has anything been explained that the Chinese deliberately leaves as an image? Third: does C ever sound like advice, or like Legge with the brackets removed? Where B already does the work, C is needless; where B still baffles, C earns its extra words. The strongest tests here are 3 line 5 (stored fat, or withheld richness?), 25 line 2 (a harvest paradox), 29’s judgment (how trust and heart connect), 55 lines 1–2 (ten days; suspicion and sickness, or dislike?), and 62 line 3 (an order to guard, or a failure to guard?).
