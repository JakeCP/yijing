# Poem fidelity review (Phase 5, Part A)

Independent slot-by-slot review of `data/poem/NN.json` against `texts.zh` in
`data/hexagrams.json`, checked against the glossary and voice rules in
[POEM_STYLE.md](POEM_STYLE.md). Only six fault types were in scope:
mistranslation, omission, added image, glossary inconsistency, slot
misalignment, and gloss-ese. Sound lines were left alone; interpretive
choices documented in a file's `notes` were kept unless the Chinese
contradicts them. Legge was not used as evidence.

## Result

- **79 slots fixed** in 44 hexagram files: added-image 11, gloss-ese 4, glossary 17, mistranslation 32, omission 15, slot misalignment 0.
- **Slot misalignment: none.** Every line statement was in its own slot in all 64.
- **Clean (no fault found):** 1, 5, 8, 13, 15, 23, 25, 30, 31, 41, 44, 45, 49, 50, 52, 53, 54, 56, 58, 64.
- Supplied pronouns for 其/之/爾 (`his`, `your` where a pronoun is present) are the
  corpus convention and were not treated as faults; second person was removed
  only where the Chinese has no pronoun or possessive at all (e.g. 27 judgment,
  35 L6, 38 L2) or a glossary-fixed string pins the wording (不可貞).
- Remaining generic `you` as a supplied verb subject (18 L4, 44 L1, 47 L6,
  62 L3, 62 L4; 40 L4 rests on the documented 而 = 爾 reading) was left for the
  lead: the style sheet prefers no supplied subject, but each line reads
  soundly and a corpus-wide sweep would be taste, not fidelity.
- After these edits, `node scripts/build.mjs` was re-run so
  `data/hexagrams.json` carries the corrected rendering (`notes` stripped),
  and `DATA_VERSION` in `js/app.js` was bumped to `3`.

## Follow-up: voice-B fidelity review (desktop-design)

Independent re-review of the voice-B rewrite (commit `975f98a`) of all 64
hexagrams (`data/poem/NN.json`) against `texts.zh` in
`data/hexagrams.json`, checked against [POEM_STYLE.md](POEM_STYLE.md) as
revised for voice B. The reviewer did not write the rendering; Legge was
not used as evidence. Eight batch reviewers covered the 64 hexagrams
between them and raised 139 candidate findings; every candidate was then
checked by the editor against the Chinese, the file's `notes`, the fixed
glossary and the fault list fixed by the voice-A review above, and seven
candidates were rejected (listed under Judgment calls below), ten were
applied in a modified form, three pairs were merged into one slot change
each, and four further faults were found on the editor's mechanical scans
(glossary/banned-word/refrain/prior-fix-regression and an A→B diff for
newly added adverbs). Only seven fault types were in scope:
mistranslation, omission, added-meaning (an image, event, object, time or
degree word, or exclusivity with no Chinese behind it), imposed-reading (a
supplied subject or *if/then/so/but* link the Chinese does not support),
glossary inconsistency, slot misalignment, and awkward English. Sound
lines were left alone, and no line was rewritten for taste. Where a verse
change removed the wording a file's `notes` defended, the note was
rewritten to match (notes are review-only and stripped at build).

Editorial line on voice B's supplied words. A supplied *he/she/you/they*
or connective was kept where the slot's story or the hexagram's running
protagonist fixes who acts (`其`/`爾` present, a named woman/wife/girl, the
waiter of 5, the litigant of 6, the traveller of 56, and the like), or
where a Chinese particle carries the relation (`則`, `乃`, `若`, `雖`, `斯`,
`而` in sequence). It was removed only where it has no antecedent and
genders or numbers a generic statement, contradicts an explicit generic
`人`, merges two people into one, genders a topic that the slot itself
then splits (wife/husband, small/noble person), enters a glossary-fixed
subjectless formula (`不可貞`, `不可涉大川`), conditionalises a fixed
verdict tag (`征凶`, `往蹇來…`, `往何咎`), contradicts its own particle
(`或` = perhaps, `既` = already), or turns mere juxtaposition into cause
(`so`) or contrast (`but`). Removing every supplied pronoun where a
narrative actor is plainly implied would have silently undone the voice-B
decision itself, so no corpus-wide pronoun sweep was made; remaining
defensible instances are noted for the lead below.

### Reviewer disagreements / judgment calls

- Rejected after checking, and left as shipped: 1 L4 'still' (voice-A
  wording; 在淵 against 躍 carries it); 8 L1 (`有孚` embedded takes 'trust'
  by syntax under the glossary entry, and the drawer-close is the
  hexagram's running actor); 22 L2 and 40 L1 (the verb/object and
  `No`/`harm` line splits are how voice B keeps a two-character line at
  two verse lines — a mechanics trade-off, not un-English); 38 L1 'bad
  people' (`惡人` is number-unmarked, so the generic plural is not an
  error); 59 L3 'his own self' (idiomatic, and the emphasis answers to
  `其躬` against the outward scatterings of L4/L6); 64 L4 'rewarded …
  with a great state' (`有賞于大國` as reward by enfeoffment with a state
  is the established reading — cf. 開國承家 at 7 L6 — so the proposed
  'reward from a great state' was not adopted; the lead may still want a
  note here, as the phrase is genuinely ambiguous).
- 46 L1 `允升`: reverted to 'He climbs in trust.' `允` can also be read
  as consent, but the passive 'is allowed' needs a permitter the text
  never names; the file note now records the rejected reading.
- 57 L5 stem-days now match 18 ('the seventh day' for `庚`, 'the first
  day' for `甲`). 49 judgment's `己日` remains 'its own day' on its
  documented note reading (the appointed day) — three treatments of
  stem-days by meaning; the lead may prefer one rule.
- Left unchanged for the lead, as documented interpretive choices rather
  than faults: 23 L1/L2 `蔑貞` as 'the casting ignored'; 26 L6 `何` read
  as `荷` 'bearing' (noted nowhere in the file — a note should be added if
  the reading stands); 55 L2 `疾` as 'ill feeling'; 56 L2 `貞` read with
  the servant ('who holds firm'); 17 L6 agency of `乃從維之`; 25 L3/L5
  'out of nowhere' for `無妄之災/疾` against 'Without falseness' for `無妄`
  elsewhere in 25.
- 47 L6 keeps the person shift inside one slot (quoted 'if I move …',
  then narrated 'if he regrets') — the Chinese (`曰動悔。有悔，征吉`) has
  the same shift from speech to verdict, but it is the most awkward
  surviving voice-B linkage and worth the lead's eye.
- Generic supplied *he* remains in many narrative line statements (the
  waiter, hunter, officer, withdrawer, stiller and similar running
  figures). This is voice B working as defined; a stricter reading of
  'no actor the story does not imply' would remove dozens more and is a
  style decision, not a fidelity fix.
